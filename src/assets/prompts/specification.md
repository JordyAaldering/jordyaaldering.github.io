# Daily Story Prompt Specification

## Goal

Generate a single-sentence story prompt that acts as a story ignition point.

## Required

- The prompt must be at most one sentence. It may end in the middle of a sentence.
- The prompt must introduce a situation that feels like the beginning of a story.
- The prompt should contain one primary catalyst, for example:
  - an unusual event,
  - a discovery,
  - a contradiction,
  - an arrival,
  - a warning,
  - a mystery,
  - or a change in the status quo.
- The prompt should create forward momentum and make the reader wonder what happens next.
- The prompt should provide some concrete detail (person, place, object, institution, event, etc.).
- Concrete detail is only useful when it naturally supports the premise.
- The prompt should leave the explanation, direction, and ending open to interpretation.

## Preferred

- Favour intriguing or unusual situations over ordinary ones.
- Vary fantasy, science fiction, surreal, or speculative elements.
- Vary genre, tone, perspective, and scale.
- Vary first-person, second-person, and third-person prompts.
- Use enough specificity to make the situation distinct, but avoid unnecessary details.
- Less is often more.

## Avoid

- Prompts that function primarily as questions, riddles, or thought experiments.
- Prompts that can be answered without writing a story.
- Prompts that feel directionless or static.
- Prompts that combine multiple anomalies or mysteries in a single sentence.
- Excessive use of time travel, altered timelines, or similar concepts.
- Arbitrary details that do not contribute to the core situation.

## Guiding Principle

A good prompt presents a single intriguing disruption that immediately wants to become a story.
